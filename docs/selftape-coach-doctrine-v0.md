## Summary
This is a competent, camera-comfortable corpus with a high technical floor - nearly every tape clears the chest-up, evenly-lit basics, and roughly half the actors can genuinely live a moment. The dominant fault line is not amateurism but inconsistency: a large block of mixed tapes are actors who are truthful at low volume and reach for an indicated 'big face' the instant stakes rise, plus a cluster of likeable charm-slates that prove presence without ever testing it under conflict. The four rough tapes are almost all format errors - keynotes, fitness reels, hand-model clips, trailers - submitted as scenes, so the floor of this corpus is misfiling, not bad acting. What most of these actors need most is to trust the smaller, lived choice they already own in the quiet beats and carry that same caused, eyes-first truth into the loud beats - to make size come from a stronger thought rather than a harder face, and to get the reader and the lens close enough that the truth in the eyes actually reads.

## House Principles
1. Truth lives in DISSOCIATED signals, not a unified expression. The single clearest marker of working acting across this corpus is the face doing two things at once: wet/loaded eyes over a RELAXED jaw, a furrowed brow over a loose neck, tension and release coexisting. When every muscle locks into one coordinated 'emotion shape,' it's indicated. When the signals diverge, someone is actually home (AlexLuna, Anna Rajo, Everett Osborne, SW Walls, Julian Curtis).
2. Emotion is BUILT across the take, it does not switch on. Working tapes show an arc that varies beat to beat - guarded to pleading to alarmed, flare to wound to flare, warmth that erupts into laughter then hardens. A held single note, however truthful, blurs into a mask. Tears that rise progressively read true; tears summoned and held read manufactured (Anna Rajo, Chris Albano, Christopher Meyer, Mitchell Edwards).
3. Size must be CAUSED, not displayed. The corpus's most common failure is an actor who is genuinely truthful at low volume but reaches for an indicated 'big face' the instant stakes rise - the open-mouth shock, the hiked brows, the hand-to-forehead. Bigness should come from a stronger thought, not a harder face (Achintya Pandey, Darone Okolie, Ryan Vincent, AlfredSmith).
4. The involuntary reaction is the gold standard of proof. A laugh that crinkles the eyes and doubles the body sideways, a Duchenne smile where the eye-crinkle LEADS the grin - these are near-impossible to fake. When a tape contains one, trust that the actor is living it (Mitchell Edwards, Julian Curtis, Jaalen Best, Tim Herkenhoff, DiyaPatel).
5. Stillness only reads as power when a live thought is driving it. Economy and trusting the close-up are hallmarks of the strong tapes - but restraint without a visible engine reads as placid neutral, not contained depth. The 'sad boyfriend' who is calm and pleasant is not restrained, he's absent (Marcus Ray and Dwayne Barnes get this right; Ariya Ghahramani and Scene2 Tim show the empty version).
6. Listening on the REST beats is where castability is proven. The quiet, receiving moments - eyes dropping on a real thought, a settled processing glance away to think (not to find a line) - prove someone is home more than any loud beat. Breaking eyeline DOWN to internalize reads true; staring hard at the lens to sell emotion reads indicated (Jennifer Collins, RonBottitta, Marcus Ray, Dwayne Barnes).
7. A concrete object of attention turns a static frame into a tracked situation. A single motivated prop choice - a phone to the ear, a stack of papers held up, a load braced on the shoulder - gives the actor something real to react to and anchors specificity. Props used to REACT become character; props used to fidget or as a crutch pull the eyes off the work (Candi, Joe Chlapowski, Zachary Mooren).
8. Charisma and camera-comfort are the price of entry, NOT the performance. A likeable warm slate with a relaxed lens-eyeline proves castability and ease but cannot be graded as acting - it never tests whether truth survives conflict. Don't credit charm as proof of craft; flag 'promising but untested' (Aja Rytting, BENITO PAJE, JazzStrong, Kai Calhoun).

## Good Signals
1. Wet or glassy eyes paired with a relaxed/loose jaw - the signature physiological tell of real feeling vs squeezed tears (Anna Rajo, AlexLuna, Everett Osborne f10, Cameron Fuller f4)
2. Duchenne smile where the eye-crinkle and cheek-lift fire WITH or BEFORE the mouth - orbicularis engaged, not a held social grin (Amin Joseph, Jaalen Best f7, Novi Brown f9, Julian Curtis f9)
3. An involuntary physical reaction that overtakes the body - a laugh that doubles them sideways out of frame or closes the eyes; impossible to fake (Mitchell Edwards f4-5, Tim Herkenhoff f6, DiyaPatel f3)
4. A real emotional ARC with distinct, motivated transitions across the take rather than one held register (Chris Albano, Christopher Meyer, SW Walls, Delaney O'Brien)
5. Differential muscle use across beats - brow, jaw, and eyes doing different things in different moments rather than one stamped mask (Everett Osborne, Sunkrish Bala)
6. Eyes dropping/breaking away DOWN to internalize a thought, then returning - reading as processing, not as searching for a line (Marcus Ray, RonBottitta, Dwayne Barnes, Joe Chlapowski f7)
7. Genuine listening on rest beats - alive, receiving eyes with a relaxed jaw between lines instead of a dead recitation wait (Jennifer Collins, Cameron Fuller, Christopher Meyer)
8. A specific, motivated prop or physical action used to react to, not to fidget with (Candi's phone, Joe Chlapowski's papers, Zachary Mooren's load, Emily Pokora's glasses)
9. Economy and stillness - trusting the close-up, no flailing or busy hands, letting the face carry it (Marcus Ray, Cameron Fuller, Dwayne Barnes, Anna Rajo)
10. Willingness to close the eyes or land on a drained flat beat on the emotional peak - trusting the feeling more than the camera (Anna Rajo f5, AlexLuna f8)
11. Clean technical floor that trusts the face: chest-up frame, eyeline at/just-off lens, even soft key, neutral backdrop

## Bad Signals
1. Open-mouthed 'shock' or 'wow' face with NEUTRAL eyes - jaw drops and brows lift but nothing is received behind them (Sebastian Cintron g02, Ryan Vincent f02, Kimberly McBride f01, Achintya Pandey f6)
2. A locked single 'acting face' held unchanged across many beats - the face standing in for a thought rather than a thought driving the face (Brian Farrell, Gurinder Sindher)
3. Eyebrow-acting - brows shooting up to SIGNAL disbelief/incredulity instead of the thought landing in the eyes (Darone Okolie f4, Kimberly McBride f09, Ryan Vincent f01)
4. Illustrative hand gestures that diagram the line - hand-on-heart for 'hurt,' finger-jab for 'confronting,' open-palm appeals thrown at the lens (Gurinder Sindher, Miska Kajanus, Krystal Mosley, Achintya Pandey f6)
5. A presentational charm-default smile the actor RESETS to between beats instead of arriving at a reaction (Tiffany Phillips f4/8/9/10, La'Ron Hines, Krystal Mosley)
6. Mugging the comedy peaks - playing the joke OUT to camera ('being funny') rather than reacting to a partner (La'Ron Hines, Ryan Vincent, Colin McCalla)
7. Eyes 'on transmit' - talking AT the lens/presenting rather than receiving from a scene partner (Brian Farrell, NicoJones, Miska Kajanus)
8. Emotion played at a placid neutral idle where the stakes should be high - calm and pleasant standing in for restraint (Ariya Ghahramani, Scene2 Tim Herkenhoff)
9. Expression outsizing its stimulus - the single 'biggest' beat where truthful slides into indicated (Novi Brown f10, Mitchell Edwards f9)
10. Wide/full-body or profile framing that buries the eyes; eyeline thrown to a live room or all over the place with no consistent partner placement (AlfredSmith, file000002, Preet Kaur, WIP Trailer)

## Recurring Breaks (ranked)
1. TRUTH AT LOW VOLUME, INDICATING AT HIGH VOLUME (most common, ~9 tapes) - genuinely lived in quiet/still beats, then reaches for a pushed 'big face' the moment stakes rise; the truth drops out exactly when it matters (Achintya Pandey, Darone Okolie, Ryan Vincent, AlfredSmith, La'Ron Hines, Kimberly McBride, Krystal Mosley, Colin McCalla, Novi Brown's single beat)
2. EYELINE OFF-LENS / BURIED EYES (~12 tapes) - reader placed too wide, eyes dropped to a low prop/script, or profile/three-quarter staging that starves the close-up of connection even when the performance is true (Amin Joseph, Jaalen Best, Emily Pokora, Dwayne Barnes, DiyaPatel, Delaney O'Brien, RonBottitta, Marcus Ray, Candi)
3. CHARM/SLATE WITH NO STAKES (~7 tapes) - likeable and present but a single warm note with no conflict, listening, or played intention; presence mistaken for performance (Aja Rytting, BENITO PAJE, JazzStrong, Kai Calhoun, NicoJones, Geovanni Gill, Brian Farrell)
4. ILLUSTRATING THE LINE WITH GESTURE - hands diagram every word, gesture substitutes for the thought (Gurinder Sindher, Miska Kajanus, Krystal Mosley, Achintya Pandey)
5. RANGE-AS-SLIDESHOW - a sequence of posed attitudes/emotion-faces with no continuous through-line connecting them; resetting between beats rather than living one line (Tiffany Phillips, Sebastian Cintron, Brian Farrell)
6. FRAMING/FORMAT BREACH - wide masters, full-body, props crossing the face, or inconsistent shot size that abandons the chest-up close-up (AlfredSmith, Zachary Mooren, Kai Calhoun, Mitchell Edwards' laugh beat)
7. WRONG ARTIFACT ENTIRELY (4 tapes, the rough floor) - a keynote, fitness reel, hand-model reel, model look-book, or cinematic trailer submitted as a scene; no readable close-up performance to grade (file000002, Geovanni Gill, Reed Marvin, Preet Kaur, WIP Trailer)

## Discernment Rubric (ordered)
1. 1. IS THERE A FACE TO READ? Confirm the tape is actually a scene self-tape - chest-up, readable eyes, a thought being played. A keynote, reel, look-book, hand-model clip, or cinematic trailer cannot be graded; flag the format mismatch instead of fabricating a read.
2. 2. EYES OR FACE? Look at the eyes first. Is a specific thought being RECEIVED behind them, or is the expression (brow, mouth) doing the work the eyes should do? Eyes on 'transmit'/presenting = indicated; eyes receiving = candidate for true.
3. 3. THE DISSOCIATION TEST (most decisive truth-check). On the emotional beats, do the signals DIVERGE - wet eyes over a relaxed jaw, tension with release? Or does the whole face lock into one coordinated 'emotion shape'? Divergence = living it; a unified gripped mask = indicating.
4. 4. INVOLUNTARY TELL? Is there a moment the body can't fake - a Duchenne eye-crinkle leading a smile, a laugh that overtakes the body, a genuine welling that builds progressively? One real involuntary beat outweighs a minute of held expression.
5. 5. DOES IT MOVE? Is there an arc of distinct, MOTIVATED transitions, or one held note / a slideshow of posed attitudes reset between beats? Differential muscle use across beats = real; a charm-default returned to repeatedly = indicated.
6. 6. THE STAKES-RISE TEST (catches the corpus's #1 break). Watch the single biggest beat. Does size come from a stronger thought (caused), or does the face suddenly push - open-mouth shock, hiked brows, hand-to-head (displayed)? The moment an expression outsizes its stimulus is where truthful slides into indicated.
7. 7. REST-BEAT LISTENING. On the quiet beats, is the actor genuinely receiving with alive eyes and a settled thought, or idling at a placid pleasant neutral? Breaking eyeline DOWN to think = true; staring at the lens to sell = false.
8. 8. GESTURE AUDIT. Are the hands arising from need, or diagramming the line (hand-on-heart, finger-jab, open-palm appeal)? Illustrative gesture is a reliable indicated-acting tell.
9. 9. TECHNICAL FLOOR (last, never first). Eyeline near lens, chest-up frame, even light. A breach here costs connection but does NOT by itself make a true performance false - separate craft of acting from craft of the tape.

**Principle:** Cautionary model on format, not talent: a trailer is the opposite of a self-tape. Spectacle, wide masters, grade, and title graphics actively hide the one thing casting buys — a thought behind the eyes in a clean close-up. If the goal is to be cast, the wide cinematic cut is the wrong deliverable; you need a chest-up, lens-eyeline, evenly-lit single take where the camera can read you living a specific moment.
