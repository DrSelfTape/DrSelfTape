Watch your tape
like casting does.

Before you send, play it back and check these six things the way a casting director would — cold, fast, looking for a reason to stop watching. Then read the last one carefully.
The 6-point self-audit
1

Does the scene start in the first 3 seconds?

No throat-clear, no "whenever you're ready." First line, first frame.

2

Is your eyeline right next to the lens?

Close and just off-center — not wandering to the corner of the room.

3

Is the frame clean?

Chest-up, small headroom, plain wall. They see your face, not your room.

4

Is the audio dry and close?

No echo, no hum. Bad sound gets a tape skipped before you've said a word.

5

Did you make one clear choice?

One specific human, fully committed — not three careful options hedged together.

6

Is the slate quick and warm?

Name, role, done. A handshake, not a second audition.

The one you can't check yourself

Are you being truthful — or indicating?

Here's the trap: you can nail all six above and still not book, because there's one thing you literally cannot judge in your own tape. You know what you meant, so your brain fills in what you meant — instead of what's actually on the screen. Casting only sees what's there. You're too close to see whether you're living the moment or just playing the shape of it.

So let something that isn't you watch it.
