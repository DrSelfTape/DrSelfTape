The 8-Second
Tape.

Casting saw 200 tapes today. Yours got 8 seconds. We studied 110 real self-tapes to find what the ones that got watched were actually doing. Here it is.
1

The scene starts in the first 3 seconds.

No throat-clear, no "whenever you're ready." Your first line is your first frame. Dead air is a skip.

→ the AI reader cues you on the line, not the silence
2

The eyeline hugs the lens.

The reader sits right beside the camera, so the thought lives in the eyes casting is looking at — not off in the corner of the room.

→ on-screen reader sits at lens height
3

They're listening, not waiting.

The reaction is booked before the line lands. You can see the thought move behind the eyes. That's the whole game.

→ Tape Review flags where you're indicating vs. truly listening
4

The reader never upstages them.

Even energy, right pace, no overacting opposite you to pull focus. A bad reader sinks a good tape.

→ the AI reader matches tone and stays out of your light
5

Clean frame: chest-up, small headroom, plain wall.

Casting has to see the face, not the bedroom behind it. The house look is boring on purpose — it puts you first.

→ built-in framing + house-look guide
6

Audio is dry and close.

Room echo and AC hum get a tape skipped in two seconds — before you've said a word worth hearing.

→ studio-clean sound, every take
7

One clear choice, fully committed.

Not three careful options hedged together — one specific human, all the way. Casting is buying conviction.

→ Compare Takes picks the one that actually lands
8

The slate is quick and confident.

Name, role, done. It's a handshake, not a monologue. Then you get out of your own way and act.

→ the house slate standard
